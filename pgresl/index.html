PG-ResL enables direction-free dexterous grasping by combining semantic reference generation, a base policy pre-trained via behaviour cloning (BC), and a residual policy trained via reinforcement learning (RL).

Abstract

Dexterous grasping with high-DoF multi-fingered hands remains challenging due to the large exploration space and the need to generalize across diverse approach directions and interaction poses. Although recent learning-based methods perform well in constrained settings such as top-down grasping, their performance often degrades when the robot must approach objects from arbitrary directions to satisfy different interaction intents.

In this work, we present PG-ResL, a prior-guided residual learning framework for robust and generalizable direction-free dexterous grasping. Given a semantic grasp intent, PG-ResL first generates a reference action sequence that provides a coarse approach-grasp-lift interaction pattern. We then introduce a reference-initialized diffusion base policy that starts denoising from a noisy version of the reference action chunk, explicitly injecting the prior into the action generation process. Finally, a residual reinforcement learning policy learns bounded corrective offsets on top of the base actions, allowing the policy to adapt to complex contact dynamics while preserving the intended grasp interaction. Extensive simulation and real-world experiments demonstrate that PG-ResL achieves strong performance in direction-free dexterous grasping and successfully transfers to physical hardware.

Method Overview

PG-ResL is a prior-guided hierarchical residual learning framework. Instead of learning a dexterous grasping policy from scratch, the method explicitly constructs a semantic-rich behavioral prior through reference action generation. The reference trajectory is segmented into action chunks and injected into a diffusion-based behavior cloning policy through reference initialization, so that the denoising process performs structured refinement around the intended approach-grasp-lift sequence.

After the diffusion-based policy is trained, its parameters are frozen, and a residual policy is optimized via reinforcement learning. The residual policy predicts fine-grained corrective actions within a localized correction space around the base actions. This design reduces the high-dimensional exploration difficulty of dexterous hands, improves robustness under contact-rich dynamics, and helps preserve the functional semantics of the target grasp.

Highlights

  • Semantic reference generation: reference action sequences are generated from text prompts and object point clouds to provide coarse approach-grasp-lift priors.
  • Reference-initialized diffusion: the diffusion base policy starts denoising from noisy reference action chunks instead of pure Gaussian noise or repeated target poses.
  • Residual policy learning: a residual RL policy learns bounded corrective offsets on top of the frozen base policy, improving execution stability and cross-object generalization.

Experiments

We evaluate PG-ResL in NVIDIA Isaac Gym under both top-down and direction-free grasping settings. In the top-down setting, the hand starts from a predefined position above the object. In the direction-free setting, the hand approaches the object from arbitrary directions on the upper hemisphere, which introduces a substantially larger exploration space.

PG-ResL achieves competitive performance in standard top-down grasping and substantially improves over strong baselines in direction-free grasping. On the direction-free setting, PG-ResL achieves 89.4% success on the training set, 87.9% on test-seen objects, 86.0% on test-unseen objects, and 84.5% on Objaverse objects. These results demonstrate strong robustness to diverse approach directions, object geometries, and dataset shift.

We further evaluate the method across diverse functional grasp poses from Dexonomy. PG-ResL consistently outperforms reference-only execution and ResDex across seven representative grasp types, showing its ability to preserve functional intent while producing physically feasible grasp motions.

Real-World Validation

To evaluate sim-to-real transfer, we deploy PG-ResL on a physical robotic platform with a 6-DoF RealMan RM75-6F arm and a 12-DoF XHand dexterous hand. The generated trajectories are retargeted from the simulated Shadow Hand to the XHand, and object poses are estimated by point cloud registration between real scans and object mesh models.

Experiments on representative household objects show that PG-ResL can execute stable real-world grasps with noisy target pose inputs and diverse semantic prompts, demonstrating the practical applicability of the proposed prior-guided residual learning framework.
